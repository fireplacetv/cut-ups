About Cut-Up Techniques

The cut-up technique was pioneered by Brion Gysin and popularized by William S. Burroughs. It involves physically cutting a text into pieces and rearranging them to create new, often surreal, combinations. This app applies these techniques digitally using only words, lines, or sentences from your input.

Quadrant Cut (2D)

How it works: The true Burroughs method: wraps your text into a 2D grid, makes a horizontal cut through the middle and a vertical cut through the middle, creating 4 quadrants. Then randomly shuffles the 4 quadrants and reassembles them. This is faithful to Burroughs's original physical technique with scissors and tape.

Example (80 char width):

Input: 
The cat sat on the mat and slept all day
long in the sun by the window sill
The dog ran in the park with friends and
played fetch all day until sunset
Output (quadrants shuffled): 
played fetch all day until sunset
The dog ran in the park with friends and
The cat sat on the mat and slept all day
long in the sun by the window sill

Fold-In

How it works: Splits the text in half by lines, then interleaves them—alternating lines from the first half and second half. This mimics Gysin's original fold-in technique where you'd fold a page in half and read across the seam.

Example:

Input (6 lines): 
Line 1
Line 2
Line 3
---
Line 4
Line 5
Line 6
Output (interleaved): 
Line 1
Line 4
Line 2
Line 5
Line 3
Line 6

Line Shuffle

How it works: Cuts your text into individual lines and randomly shuffles them. A looser technique that destroys paragraph structure while keeping each line intact.

Example:

Input: 
The cat sat on the mat.
The dog ran in the park.
The bird flew in the sky.
Output (lines shuffled): 
The bird flew in the sky.
The cat sat on the mat.
The dog ran in the park.

Sentence Shuffle

How it works: Cuts your text into sentences (split on . ! ?) and randomly shuffles them. Preserves more grammatical structure than line shuffle while still creating unexpected juxtapositions.

Example:

Input: 
The cat sat. The dog ran. The bird flew.
Output (sentences shuffled): 
The dog ran. The bird flew. The cat sat.

Word Scramble

How it works: Cuts your text into individual words and randomly shuffles them. The most extreme technique—destroys grammar and word order, often creating pure chaos or unexpected poetry. This is similar to techniques Burroughs used in his most experimental work.

Example:

Input: 
The cat sat on the mat
Output (words scrambled): 
mat on The cat the sat

Tips for Experimentation

  • Layer techniques: Use "Use Result" to load output back into input and apply different methods sequentially. Word Scramble → Sentence Shuffle can create interesting effects.
  • Try different source texts: Poetry works well, but try prose, lists, or even mixed media (recipes, instructions, song lyrics).
  • Random Wikipedia: Click "🎲 Random Wikipedia" to pull in a random section of a random Wikipedia article as source text—an easy way to get varied, real-world prose to cut up.
  • Adjust line width: Experiment with different character widths to see how the Quadrant cut changes with different text layouts.
  • Wrap Text: Use the "Wrap Text" button to reflow your text at the current line width independently of any cut-up method—handy for prepping text before cutting, or cleaning up ragged output afterward.
  • Clean Whitespace: Some methods (Quadrant especially) can leave wide gaps between words or too many blank lines. Use the "Clean Whitespace" button to re-wrap paragraphs at the current line width and collapse those gaps, while still preserving paragraph breaks.
  • Embrace chance: The unexpected juxtapositions are the point. Keep clicking to explore different cuts.
